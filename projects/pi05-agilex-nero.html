Fine-Tuning Pi0.5 on AgileX Nero

Abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation models represent a transformative shift in robotic learning, enabling robots to interpret visual scenes and natural language instructions to generate continuous motor actions. However, deploying pre-trained foundation models onto specialized physical hardware often requires efficient domain adaptation to address specific kinematic configurations, gripper geometries, and environmental dynamics.

This project implements the fine-tuning of Physical Intelligence's π0.5 (Pi0.5) foundation model for autonomous robotic pick-and-place manipulation on the AgileX Nero manipulator arm. Utilizing demonstration episodes collected across varied tabletop positions, the pre-trained flow-based VLA policy was adapted using the OpenPI framework to map visual observations and task instructions into continuous joint and gripper control. Hardware evaluations demonstrate robust visual grounding, precise grasp approach, and reliable placement onto the target plate in real-world tabletop experiments.

Methodology

The adaptation pipeline spans data collection, policy fine-tuning with OpenPI, and real-time physical robot deployment:

Robotic Hardware & Sensory Setup

  • Manipulator: AgileX Nero 6-DoF lightweight robotic arm with parallel two-finger gripper.
  • Perception: Multi-view RGB camera setup including an overhead third-person camera and wrist-mounted camera for close-range manipulation.
  • Environment: Tabletop workstation located at the Robert Bosch Centre for Cyber-Physical Systems (RBCCPS).
  • Control Interface: High-frequency joint position and gripper action streaming over CAN/Ethernet bus.

π0.5 VLA Architecture & Fine-Tuning

  • Base Model: Physical Intelligence's π0.5 foundation model trained on diverse cross-embodiment robotic datasets.
  • Action Representation: Flow-based continuous action chunking predicting multi-step joint trajectories conditioned on vision and language tokens.
  • Training Framework: Fine-tuned via the OpenPI codebase using domain-specific teleoperated demonstrations.
  • Domain Adaptation: Fine-tuned model weights to specialize the general visuomotor representations to AgileX Nero's joint limits and gripper actions.

Pick-and-Place Task Execution

  • Task Prompt: Visual and language conditioning guiding the robot to pick the target red cube and place it on the white plate.
  • Visual Grounding: Policy reliably attends to the red block irrespective of minor background clutter and lighting shifts.
  • Smooth Trajectories: Generates continuous velocity profiles avoiding sudden acceleration peaks or gripper slippage.
  • Closed-Loop Reactive Control: Evaluated in real time at 30 Hz for closed-loop visual servoing and grasp correction.

Key Innovations

Foundation Model Adaptation

Demonstrated successful parameter fine-tuning of the state-of-the-art π0.5 VLA model on custom AgileX robotic hardware.

End-to-End Visuomotor Policy

Direct mapping from raw RGB camera frames and language commands to continuous joint commands without hand-engineered primitives.

Sample Efficiency

Leveraged pre-trained physical priors from Physical Intelligence to achieve task mastery with a compact demonstration dataset.

High Success Rate

Achieved robust pick-and-place performance with reliable grasping, stable object transport, and accurate plate placement.

Impact and Future Directions

This deployment demonstrates the feasibility of bringing general-purpose Vision-Language-Action foundation models to compact, accessible robotic platforms:

Accessible Embodied AI

Validates high-capability VLA deployment on cost-effective, compact arm platforms like AgileX Nero.

Open-Source Robotics

Utilizes the OpenPI stack for standardized data logging, model fine-tuning, and real-time inference.

Generalizable Manipulation

Establishes the foundation for multi-task multi-object manipulation with diverse language instructions.

Future Enhancements

  • Multi-task expansion to diverse everyday objects, tools, and kitchenware
  • Bimanual coordination using dual AgileX arms for cooperative manipulation
  • Integration of tactile sensing feedback into the policy observation space
  • Long-horizon task chaining with high-level visual language planning

Conclusion

Fine-tuning the π0.5 Vision-Language-Action model on the AgileX Nero manipulator demonstrates the power of modern foundation models for robotic manipulation. By leveraging pre-trained physical world representations and specializing them via OpenPI, the system achieves fluid, accurate, and reactive pick-and-place performance from raw camera streams. This work reinforces the paradigm of adapting large-scale embodied foundation models to real-world robotic systems with high sample efficiency and dependable physical execution.
